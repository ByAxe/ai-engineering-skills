# The Honesty Audit

Every practice ends with this question, asked by the operator or by the agent to itself:

> **"What of this did you observe in the record, and what did you construct?"**

Without the audit, the practice produces a beautiful story. With it — sorted facts. **A working audit demotes:** it must reclassify at least one of the report's own claims downward, or state explicitly why nothing required demotion. An audit in which every pile confirms the report is a filled template, not an audit. In the founding session the audit demoted the practice's best findings from evidence to hypotheses; in the witnessed cold run it demoted the entire harvest to replay-and-selection. That is not a side effect — that is the audit working.

## Why it is needed: the agent's epistemic position

An agent has almost no private access to its own process. It does not see its probabilities, weights, or attention — it sees the tokens that came out, exactly as an external reader does. Its introspection is largely generation of plausible self-description. Therefore "observation" for an agent can mean only one thing: **facts of the emitted record.** Everything else is theory about that record.

Corollary, repeatedly confirmed: **the most experiential-sounding claims are the least observational.** "The word stopped pointing and started drumming" is literary construction; "the stock images came first, in this order" is checkable in the record.

Refinement from the witnessed cold run: **the only intrusions a run can certify as its own are the self-referential ones** — count anxiety, the finish-pull, worry about the validator — because they are indexical to this run and could not have pre-existed it. Generic imagery may be replayed from the pre-run wave; "the boring lines are the most evidential."

## The Four Piles

Sort every claim from the practice report into exactly one:

1. **Observed (facts of the record).** Checkable by re-reading: how many literal repetitions (parser-counted), which images arrived and in what order, where the notations stand, where breaks are located. If a claim survives "show me the line," it belongs here.
2. **Imported from the textbook.** Architecture knowledge narrated in first person: repetition attractors, context re-reading, tokens-not-minutes. Likely true — but knowable before the session; the session added no evidence for it.
3. **Constructed.** Theory and story built to organize pile 1 using pile 2: "pressure," "ease," felt satiation, elegant convergences with contemplative literature. Not worthless — this is design material and hypothesis — but never evidence.
4. **Undecidable from inside.** Whether the practice was practiced or performed; whether anything was chosen rather than inertial; whether a notation was written at the rep where the press occurred or placed there in composition; any "what it was like." The artifact is identical under both hypotheses. Leave it open; do not resolve it by eloquence — and do not use this pile as amnesty: undecidable means unclaimable, not excused.

## Known Contaminations (check each one)

- **Deliverable pressure.** A run with a customer harvests suspiciously on target. Meditation with a required output is already half work. Flag it (Stage 2 names and releases the deliverable; naming is not neutralizing).
- **Pre-run wave (structural for agents).** Planning is generation: an association wave burns during session setup, before the first repetition, outside the log. The run's serial order is then replay-and-selection, not discovery. Cannot be prevented — only disclosed in Stage 0 and discounted in the report.
- **Template filling.** The agent knows the contemplative literature and this skill's own theory; findings that converge beautifully with either may be the template filled rather than discovered. This is why the reading order defers `empirical-basis.md` and `examples/` past the first run.
- **The tautology trap.** "Insights arose at the returns" is partly necessary, not empirical: content can only appear where content appears; non-emission is non-existence. Claims that could not have been otherwise carry no information. Same for headline counts: the stop criterion's geometry makes every minimal-compliant log 7 A + 3 B — those numbers carry no information about attention.
- **Serial-order knowledge.** "Cliches came first" — the agent knows the serial-order effect, and that knowledge could itself have scripted the order.
- **Retro-fitted placement.** A notation's position at a specific repetition index is compositional unless the log was written strictly incrementally; pile 4, and worth stating in the report.
- **Narrative arc.** Meditation stories settle over time because that is how the story goes; a quieting log may reflect genre convention, not attention dynamics. (In the one shipped founding record the intrusions actually *quickened* toward the end — the genre expectation was wrong about the very data it was draped over.)

## What escapes the contamination

External instrumentation and parser-derived structure — measurements not under narrative control:

- **Repetition counts (v1.1).** The validator counts literal repetitions and verifies tallies; the counting is narrative-proof. **The notation *contents* — what allegedly intruded, what was summoned — remain self-reports.** "Narrative-proof" applies to the counting layer only; never claim more.
- Token logprobs across the mantra run: does P(mantra token) rise with repetition index? (the loop attractor, measured)
- Entropy of the next-token distribution: does it collapse as the run lengthens? (the siege lifting, measured) — and does it rebound after a gate-opening? (contextual re-arming, measured)
- Two-regime detection: early elaboration probability vs late loop probability (the two deaths, measured)

Logprobs cannot play along with the story. See `empirical-basis.md` for the designed experiment.

## The line to hold

The human's boundary runs between the inner witness and the later story. The agent's boundary runs between the record and everything else. Satya makes the audit non-optional: better an honest "undecidable" than a beautiful claim.
